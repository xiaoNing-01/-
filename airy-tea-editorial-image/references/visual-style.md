# Visual style reference

## Style thesis

A calm, airy contemporary tea/editorial system: warm paper-like light, botanical
chromatic restraint, hand-made flat illustration, and typography treated as a quiet
architectural element. The appeal comes from the relationship between soft color,
large empty fields, simple shapes, and small moments of wit—not from any one product
or brand asset.

## Persistent parameters

### Color

- Base: warm milk/off-white rather than clinical white.
- Dominant: muted tea/botanical green, with a few neighboring sage or olive values.
- Structure: deep charcoal/ink for outlines and type, not absolute black.
- Accent: one small low-saturation warm accent such as apricot, amber, terracotta,
  or pale yellow. Use it as a signal, not a second dominant color.
- Keep most of the canvas quiet: approximately 65–80% base, 15–30% green family,
  2–8% accent/ink details. Adjust for legibility, not as a rigid formula.
- Avoid neon, high-chroma candy colors, metallic effects, blue-white corporate
  palettes, and rainbow decoration.

### Illustration and material

- Use simplified flat shapes with gentle irregularity: slightly imperfect curves,
  hand-drawn contours, restrained line variation, and a screen-print/editorial feel.
- Add fine paper grain or a dry pigment/fiber texture at low contrast. Texture should
  be felt at close range and disappear at thumbnail size.
- Use minimal shading: broad tonal overlap or one soft offset layer is preferable to
  realistic modeling. Keep edges mostly clean.
- Make objects symbolic and lightly playful, but avoid childish cartooning, glossy
  vector polish, 3D rendering, photorealism, and generic icon libraries.

### Composition and whitespace

- Let the empty background participate in the design. Large unoccupied areas are a
  feature, not unfinished space.
- Prefer an asymmetric, editorial arrangement with a stable visual counterweight:
  one hero object, one smaller companion, and a few tiny marks or botanical hints.
- Use generous outer margins and deliberate off-center placement. Avoid centered
  product shots, full-bleed clutter, repetitive pattern fills, and evenly spaced
  object grids.
- Establish a clear reading path between image and text. A text zone may be above,
  beside, or diagonally opposed to the hero object, but keep it calm and uncluttered.
- Favor vertical 4:5 or square compositions for social/editorial use; adapt the same
  rhythm to landscape by widening the quiet field rather than adding decoration.

### Typography mood

- Typography should feel modern, rounded, humanist, and quietly confident—closer to
  a friendly contemporary sans-serif than a formal serif or exaggerated display face.
- Use sparse copy, short lines, comfortable line spacing, generous tracking, and a
  strong but not aggressive hierarchy. Deep ink or dark green provides contrast.
- Treat type as part of the composition, not a caption pasted on afterward.
- For generated images, reserve a clean area for typesetting whenever wording must be
  exact. Do not rely on the model to produce long Chinese copy.

## Adaptation rules

Keep these properties stable across unrelated subjects:

1. base-to-color ratio and low-contrast atmosphere;
2. flat, tactile, lightly imperfect illustration finish;
3. asymmetric hierarchy and generous negative space;
4. restrained rounded sans-serif typographic temperament;
5. one small accent and a limited number of visual elements.

Change these properties to fit the topic:

- the hero metaphor and object vocabulary;
- the supporting detail and gesture;
- the semantic meaning of the accent color;
- the copy, crop, and aspect ratio;
- the exact placement, so outputs remain original rather than templated copies.

## Quality checklist

- Can the theme be understood from one hero metaphor?
- Does the background have enough air to feel intentional?
- Is green the visual anchor without becoming a flat monochrome wash?
- Is the accent small and purposeful?
- Do contours and grain feel made rather than algorithmically glossy?
- Is the type zone legible and spacious?
- Would the image still be distinctive if all text were removed?
- Does it avoid recognizable brand assets or a reference's exact composition?
